\section{Background: Where EM Lives}

\subsection{Gradient-Responsibility Identity}

For an LSE objective over distances,
\[
    L_{\mathrm{LSE}}(d) = -\log \sum_j \exp(-d_j),
    \qquad r = \operatorname{softmax}(-d),
\]
the gradient with respect to the distance coordinate is
\[
    \frac{\partial L_{\mathrm{LSE}}}{\partial d_j} = r_j.
\]
For cross-entropy classification,
\[
    L = -h_y + \log \sum_k \exp(h_k),
    \qquad
    \frac{\partial L}{\partial h_j}
    = \operatorname{softmax}(h)_j - \mathbf{1}[j=y].
\]
The output softmax is therefore a label-clamped responsibility interface.

\subsection{Locality}

The identity applies where the loss sees distances or energies. It does not
make all earlier hidden layers into mixture models. The output loss geometry
imposes EM structure at the output interface, and gradients from that structure
flow backward to shape internal representations. But by the time the gradient
has passed through intervening Jacobians, it is no longer a posterior over the
earlier layer's units.

\subsection{Volume Control}

The decoder-free encoder study used volume control to prevent the standard
degeneracies of mixture models. The LSE term creates competition and
responsibility structure. The variance term prevents collapsed or dead
components. The decorrelation term prevents redundant components. Together they
play the neural role of a log-determinant barrier.
