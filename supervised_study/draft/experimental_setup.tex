\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The experiments use deliberately small supervised networks. The goal is not to
improve MNIST accuracy, but to make the location and failure mode of implicit EM
measurable. The architecture is kept fixed across experiments except for the
controlled ablations described below.

\subsection{Model Family}

The base model is a two-layer MNIST classifier with an intermediate component
site:
\[
    x \in \mathbb{R}^{784}
    \rightarrow
    \operatorname{Linear}(784,K)
    \rightarrow
    \operatorname{ReLU}
    \rightarrow
    d
    \rightarrow
    \mathcal{A}(d)
    \rightarrow
    \operatorname{Linear}(K,10)
    \rightarrow
    \operatorname{LayerNorm}
    \rightarrow
    L_{\mathrm{CE}}.
\]
Here $K$ is the number of intermediate components, $d$ is the vector of
intermediate energies, and $\mathcal{A}$ is the ablated intermediate transform.
Depending on the experiment, $\mathcal{A}$ is either the identity map or
NegLogSoftmin:
\[
    \operatorname{NLS}(d)_j
    =
    d_j+\log\sum_k\exp(-d_k).
\]
When NLS is present, the site has the LSE partition function and the competitive
Jacobian needed for a local implicit-EM interpretation. When NLS is absent, the
same downstream classifier receives the raw ReLU-produced coordinates.
Experiment~1 includes a volume-control arm without NLS to separate the effect of
the calibrated NegLogSoftmin activation format from generic variance and
decorrelation regularization.

The second linear map, $W_2$, is the learned dense corridor from intermediate
components to class logits. It is the map whose pullback destroys the uniform
conditioning guarantee in Proposition~\ref{prop:composition}. Because $d$ is
produced by a ReLU after the first affine map, the trained model falls under the
smooth/ReLU kernel caveat following Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth-kernel}, not
the clean private-affine case alone. The dead-unit metric below is therefore
also a ReLU absorbing-state diagnostic. LayerNorm is kept because it stabilizes
the logits seen by CE in the supervised baseline; the clean theory omits it, and
the curvature measurements include its effect.

\subsection{Local Auxiliary Objective}

The local EM objective is applied to the intermediate distances $d$. In the
causal experiment it is
\[
    L_{\mathrm{aux}}(d)
    =
    L_{\mathrm{LSE}}(d)
    + L_{\mathrm{var}}(d)
    + L_{\mathrm{tc}}(d).
\]
The variance term $L_{\mathrm{var}}$ is the anti-collapse term: it penalizes
components whose activations have too little variance over the batch. The
decorrelation term $L_{\mathrm{tc}}$ is the anti-redundancy term: it penalizes
correlated component responses. Together they implement the volume-control
mechanism tested in the decoder-free encoder study and are related to the
variance and redundancy-reduction terms used in VICReg and Barlow Twins
\citep{bardes2022vicreg,zbontar2021barlow}.

The supervised objective is
\[
    L_{\mathrm{total}}
    =
    L_{\mathrm{CE}}
    + \lambda L_{\mathrm{aux}},
\]
with $\lambda=0.001$ in the initial ablation and capacity experiments unless
otherwise stated. In Experiments 1--3, the auxiliary loss
uses the NLS and volume-control terms specified by each ablation. In
Experiment 4, the LSE term is included explicitly so that the stop-gradient arm
trains $W_1$ by the same local EM objective as the single-layer model.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Volume-Control Ablation}

Experiment 1 tests which pieces of the intermediate EM site are needed. The
architecture uses $K=25$ components, MNIST, 50 training epochs, Adam with
learning rate $10^{-3}$, $\lambda=0.001$, and 10 seeds
$(42,\ldots,51)$. Seeds control random initialization, minibatch order, and any
probe-training randomness in experiments that use probes. Metrics are averaged
over the final 10 epochs and evaluated on the test set unless otherwise stated.

The five configurations are:
\[
\begin{array}{lccc}
\toprule
\text{Configuration} & \text{NLS} & L_{\mathrm{var}} & L_{\mathrm{tc}} \\
\midrule
\text{Baseline} & - & - & - \\
\text{NLS only} & \checkmark & - & - \\
\text{NLS + Var} & \checkmark & \checkmark & - \\
\text{NLS + Var + Decorr} & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark \\
\text{Var + Decorr only} & - & \checkmark & \checkmark \\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
This ablation separates three questions: whether supervision alone prevents
intermediate collapse, whether competition without volume control is enough,
and whether volume control is merely regularization or specifically stabilizes
an EM site.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Capacity Sweep}

Experiment 2 tests whether the at-site repair persists across width. It compares
the baseline against NLS plus full volume control at
\[
    K \in \{16,25,36,49,64\}.
\]
Each model is trained on MNIST for 50 epochs with Adam at learning rate
$10^{-3}$, $\lambda=0.001$, and 5 seeds $(42,\ldots,46)$. Metrics
are again evaluated on the test set and averaged over the final 10 epochs. This
experiment is used to
distinguish structural health from benchmark accuracy: volume control can
eliminate dead units and reduce redundancy even when the fixed regularization
weight is not accuracy-optimal at larger widths.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Optimizer Sweep}

Experiment 3 asks whether the single-site optimization signature survives
ordinary supervised composition. It fixes the NLS + full-volume-control model
with $K=25$ and $\lambda=0.001$, and sweeps optimizer and learning
rate:
\[
    \text{optimizer} \in \{\mathrm{SGD},\mathrm{Adam}\},
    \qquad
    \eta \in \{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\}.
\]
Each setting is trained for 50 epochs on MNIST with 3 seeds $(42,43,44)$.
Reported metrics are evaluated on the test set and averaged over the final 10
epochs.
The comparison is deliberately aligned with the single-layer implicit-EM
signature: learning-rate sensitivity, Adam advantage, and whether lower
auxiliary loss corresponds to better features.

\subsection{Experiment 4: Mechanism and Causal Test}

Experiment 4 measures the mechanism behind the optimizer result. The model is
\[
    x \rightarrow \operatorname{Linear}(784,25)
    \rightarrow \operatorname{ReLU}
    \rightarrow d
    \rightarrow \operatorname{NLS}(d)
    \rightarrow \operatorname{Linear}(25,10)
    \rightarrow \operatorname{LayerNorm}
    \rightarrow L_{\mathrm{CE}},
\]
with
\[
    L_{\mathrm{aux}}(d)
    =
    L_{\mathrm{LSE}}(d)+L_{\mathrm{var}}(d)+L_{\mathrm{tc}}(d).
\]
Four arms differ only in how the supervised and local objectives reach $W_1$:
\[
\begin{array}{lcc}
\toprule
\text{Arm} & \text{Total loss} & \text{CE reaches } W_1? \\
\midrule
\lambda=0.001 \text{ joint} & L_{\mathrm{CE}}+0.001L_{\mathrm{aux}} & \checkmark \\
\lambda=0.03 \text{ joint} & L_{\mathrm{CE}}+0.03L_{\mathrm{aux}} & \checkmark \\
\lambda=1 \text{ joint} & L_{\mathrm{CE}}+L_{\mathrm{aux}} & \checkmark \\
\text{stop-gradient} & L_{\mathrm{CE}}(\operatorname{head}(y.\mathrm{detach}()))
    +L_{\mathrm{aux}} & - \\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
The stop-gradient arm keeps the same data, labels, architecture, and supervised
head training, but prevents the CE gradient from reaching $W_1$. This makes it
the causal control for the corridor.

For the conditioning sweep, each arm is trained with SGD at
\[
    \eta \in \{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\}
\]
for 40 epochs and 3 seeds. Feature quality is measured after training by a
fixed-protocol linear probe trained on the frozen intermediate distances $d$.
The probe is a single linear classifier trained with Adam at learning rate
$10^{-3}$ for 15 epochs, batch size 256, with its own fixed random seed. This
decouples the measured representation from how well the supervised head trained
at a particular learning rate \citep{alain2016understanding}.

For gradient competition, the training loop measures
\[
    \|\nabla_d L_{\mathrm{CE}}\|,
    \qquad
    \lambda\|\nabla_d L_{\mathrm{aux}}\|,
    \qquad
    \cos(\nabla_d L_{\mathrm{CE}},\nabla_d L_{\mathrm{aux}}).
\]
These measurements are taken during Adam training at learning rate
$10^{-3}$, while the conditioning sweep uses SGD. We therefore report these
ratios as a site-level gradient diagnostic rather than as a matched-optimizer
measurement; the matched-SGD version is not reported here.

For curvature tracking, power iteration with exact Hessian-vector products
estimates the spectral norm of the Hessian with respect to $d$ for both the LSE
term and the CE corridor through NLS and the head. Measurements use a fixed
512-example batch every 2 epochs, and $\sigma_{\max}(W_2)$ is recorded
alongside the curvature estimates \citep{pearlmutter1994fast}.

For the private-affine scale check in Proposition~\ref{prop:private-affine}, we
also compute the data constant $\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{x}\|^2$ using the raw
$[0,1]$ MNIST pixels with a bias coordinate. Results~III compares the implied
clean-LSE descent scale to the stop-gradient learning-rate sweep.

\subsection{Metrics}

Representation health is measured by four quantities. A dead unit is a component
whose activation variance is below $0.01$ over the evaluation batch. Minimum
variance records the weakest component variance. Redundancy is
$\|\operatorname{Corr}(d)-I\|_{F,\mathrm{off}}^2$, the squared Frobenius norm of
the off-diagonal correlation matrix among component responses. Responsibility
entropy is the mean sample entropy
\[
    \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N
    \left[-\sum_{j=1}^K r_{ij}\log(r_{ij}+10^{-8})\right],
    \qquad r_i=\operatorname{softmax}(-d_i),
\]
and measures how sharp the local component assignments are.

Optimization conditioning is measured by learning-rate spread, convergence-time
stability, Adam versus SGD gaps, gradient dominance ratios, cosine similarity
between CE and auxiliary gradients, and Hessian spectral norms with respect to
the intermediate distances. For cosine measurements, we compare the observed
values to the random-vector scale $1/\sqrt{K}$, not merely to zero.
For convergence time, the reported LSE-stabilization metric is interpreted as an
EM-site stability measure; in CE-dominated arms the classifier may train well
even if the local LSE site does not stabilize.

\subsection{Experimental Logic}

The experiments follow the paper's taxonomy. Experiments 1--2 test at-site
failures: collapse, dead units, and redundancy. Their question is whether local
volume control repairs these failures inside a supervised network. Experiment 3
tests whether the single-site conditioning signature survives ordinary
supervised composition. Experiment 4 separates structure from dominance: the
dense corridor creates an ill-conditioned CE-corridor contribution, while the
measured CE/EM gradient ratio determines whether that contribution controls the
effective landscape.
