\section{Theory: The Conditioning Boundary}

\subsection{The Simplex Invariant}

\begin{lemma}[Simplex invariant and curvature bound]
For
\[
    L_{\mathrm{LSE}}(d) = -\log \sum_j \exp(-d_j),
    \qquad r = \operatorname{softmax}(-d),
\]
the gradient $\nabla_d L_{\mathrm{LSE}}=r$ lies on the probability simplex, and
the Hessian is
\[
    H_d L_{\mathrm{LSE}}
    = \operatorname{diag}(r) - rr^\top,
    \qquad
    \|H_d L_{\mathrm{LSE}}\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The proof is the standard covariance form of the multinomial-logistic Hessian,
together with the Bohning or Popoviciu bound. The important point here is that
the bound is uniform in $d$ and independent of parameters.

\subsection{Private Parameters Inherit the Bound}

\begin{proposition}[Conditioning under private energies]
Let each component energy be a private affine map
\[
    d_j = w_j^\top x + b_j.
\]
Then
\[
    \|\nabla_\theta L_{\mathrm{LSE}}\|_2 \le \|x\|,
    \qquad
    \|H_\theta L_{\mathrm{LSE}}\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2}\|x\|^2.
\]
Under batch-mean training,
\[
    \|H_\theta L_{\mathrm{LSE}}\|_2
    \le \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|x\|^2,
\]
a data constant independent of the current parameters.
\end{proposition}

This proposition explains the single-layer optimizer signature: the admissible
learning-rate range does not shrink as parameters move, curvature is not learned
or anisotropic, and an adaptive optimizer has little to correct.

\begin{proposition}[Smooth monotone kernels]
For $d_j=\phi(w_j^\top x+b_j)$ with $\phi$ monotone and $|\phi'|,|\phi''|$
bounded, the same parameter-independence survives up to constants determined by
the kernel bounds.
\end{proposition}

Softplus is the main smooth case. ReLU satisfies the curvature bounds almost
everywhere but has a separate absorbing-state pathology: once a unit receives
zero gradient, it may never recover.

\subsection{Composition Replaces the Constant With a Learned Spectrum}

\begin{proposition}[Loss of conditioning under learned composition]
Let
\[
    y = \operatorname{NLS}(d), \qquad h = W_2 y, \qquad
    L_{\mathrm{CE}} = \operatorname{CE}(h,c),
\]
and $p=\operatorname{softmax}(h)$. Then the Hessian of $L_{\mathrm{CE}}$ with
respect to $d$ contains the conjugated softmax Hessian
\[
    J^\top W_2^\top(\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top)W_2J,
\]
plus curvature terms from the NLS map. Consequently,
\[
    \|H_d L_{\mathrm{CE}}\|_2 = O(\sigma_{\max}(W_2)^2).
\]
\end{proposition}

The uniform $1/2$ bound of the local LSE site is replaced by a learned,
time-varying, anisotropic quantity. The theorem is stated without LayerNorm for
clarity; empirical curvature measurements use the actual model including
LayerNorm, so the reported constants absorb its effect.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The propositions give sufficient conditions for preserving EM conditioning and
demonstrate loss of conditioning when a shared sign-indefinite learned map
intervenes. They do not prove necessity for all possible architectures, nor do
they provide a convergence proof for the composed system.

\subsection{Operations That Preserve or Destroy the Invariant}

The invariant is preserved by private per-component parameters,
per-coordinate monotone maps, bounded smooth kernels, and stochastic or Markov
maps at least at first order. It is destroyed by dense sign-indefinite learned
maps, objective mixing, hard gates, and shared lower parameters.

\subsection{Classical EM Connection}

Classical EM's efficiency comes from the E-step making the expected
complete-data objective separate over component-private parameters. With
private component parameters $\theta_j$, the objective has the form
\[
    Q(\theta) = \sum_j Q_j(\theta_j),
\]
and the M-step decouples into independent per-component problems. If a lower
map is shared by all components, this separation disappears and the M-step
becomes a coupled generalized-EM problem. Backpropagation through shared lower
layers is the neural version of this classical failure.
