**IOU 2 — the formal sufficient condition.** Delivered, with one wrinkle. The scope paragraph lists "private component parameters, an unmixed LSE objective, and monotone per-coordinate kernels." But check Prop \ref{prop:smooth-kernel}: the proof never uses φ′ ≥ 0. Monotonicity buys you sign/component-identity preservation (the first-order invariant), not the curvature bound — the bound needs only |φ′| ≤ B₁, |φ″| ≤ B₂. So either drop monotonicity from the curvature proposition's hypotheses and keep it in the "preserving operations" taxonomy where it belongs, or explain in one line that it's assumed for the invariant, not the bound. **IOU 3 — affine qualifier.** Delivered cleanly in Prop \ref{prop:private-affine}. Now propagate it backward: the abstract still says "private component parameters" without "affine."

**The exposure I'd fix before submission.** Proposition \ref{prop:private-affine} explains the optimizer signature via the LSE curvature bound — but the single-layer model wasn't trained on L_LSE alone; it was trained on L_LSE + L_var + L_tc (it had to be — note that L_LSE is *concave* in d, so pure descent on it drives all distances down without bound; volume control is what makes the problem well-posed at all). The variance and decorrelation terms have their own curvature, and a decorrelation penalty on activations is typically quartic in W₁, whose Hessian *is* parameter-dependent. So the clean claim "the smoothness constant is fixed before training starts" holds for the LSE term but not obviously for the full objective that actually produced the observed optimizer signature. This doesn't break the paper — the empirical signature is what it is, and the LSE term may dominate the curvature in practice — but the theory-to-experiment bridge has a gap here, and it's the same *kind* of gap the paper criticizes (a foreign term whose curvature isn't LSE-controlled, sitting inside the "well-conditioned" objective). Options: bound the volume-control curvature too, measure it and show it's small, or scope the claim explicitly ("the LSE term's curvature is parameter-independent; Section X measures the full auxiliary curvature"). Any of the three works; silence doesn't. Also worth noting the concavity point somewhere — readers who notice L_LSE is concave will briefly think the smoothness story is vacuous until they remember the volume terms.
