**IOU scorecard**

- **Cosine baseline (1/√K):** Delivered, and honestly — "should not be oversold as special high-dimensional orthogonality" is the right disclaimer. But notice what your own numbers now say: observed cosines of ±0.01 are *far below* the random-direction scale of 0.2. Two unrelated random directions in K=25 would typically show cosines of order ±0.2; yours are 20× smaller. That's not "consistent with no relationship" — it's *more orthogonal than chance*, which is itself a structural fact wanting explanation. Moderate-confidence guess at the source: batch averaging (the cosine of batch-mean gradients concentrates tighter than per-example directions), or the NLS Jacobian's kernel structure constraining where each gradient can live. Worth one sentence either explaining it or flagging it as unexplained — because as written, a careful reviewer will notice the numbers undershoot your own null model and wonder if you did.
- **Matched-SGD ratio:** Still disclosed-not-done, and now the abstract's headline number rests on it. Same recommendation as before: this is a logging change, not an experiment. Run it.
- **E‖x̃‖² prediction:** Missing entirely. The theory section explicitly promised "the experiments therefore report E‖x̃‖² and compare this scale to the learning-rate tolerance observed in the stop-gradient sweep." This section is where that check lives, and it isn't here. Either add it (it's a one-number computation plus a comparison to the stop-gradient arm's stable-η range) or delete the promise from the theory section. A promised quantitative prediction that silently vanishes is the kind of thing that costs trust with exactly the reviewers you most want to impress.
- **½-saturation diagnostic:** It finally gets its meaning here (0.4993–0.4997 saturation = sharpened mixture with two-way boundary ties — and that interpretation via the Popoviciu equality case is lovely, it connects the tightness condition of Lemma 1 to an observed training endpoint). **New issues, in order of importance**

1. **The one-sided sensitivity problem from Results II recurs in the spread metric.** The joint λ=0.001 spread of 10.9 is again driven by the 10⁻⁴ arm being undertrained (85.3 climbing to 96.2 monotonically). No arm in the table hits an instability edge at 10⁻¹. So "spread" currently measures convergence speed, and the EM arms' flatness at ~88% could partly reflect a lower, easier-to-reach ceiling. The stop-gradient result is still meaningful — flat across 1000× is the single-layer signature — but the comparison would be much harder to argue with if (a) the sweep extended upward until the joint arm actually destabilizes, and (b) you reported time-to-95%-of-final probe accuracy per arm, which is the fixed-convergence-time claim in its native units. You already collect a version of this for L_LSE; the probe analogue completes it.

2. **The LSE-stabilization metric has a self-normalization problem.** "Epoch at which test L_LSE reaches 95% of its final value" — normalizing each run to its own final value means a run whose LSE barely moves can look "fast" and an erratic run's number depends on where it happens to end. The erratic joint-arm values (15, then 40, then 37.7) may reflect different final values as much as different dynamics. An absolute threshold (epoch when L_LSE first falls below a fixed value) or the trajectory plots themselves would carry the point more safely. Also, the missing λ=0.03 and λ=1 rows are disclosed, which is good — but λ=1 is the arm where this metric would be most probative (does a live-but-dominant EM path stabilize the site like stop-gradient does?). If the logs allow recomputing it, do.

3. **The ledger's 8-for-8 checkmark column.** Reviewers instinctively discount perfect prediction ledgers. The correction footnote (1000× → 30–70×) helps a lot — it's evidence the ledger is honest — but consider adding the predictions that were *not* confirmed or not yet tested (Adam-under-stop-gradient, matched-SGD, E‖x̃‖²) as open rows with "untested" verdicts. A ledger with open cells reads as an instrument; a ledger with only ✓ reads as marketing. Also "the most visible Paper 2 signature" — "Paper 2" is an internal name leaking through; use the citation.

4. **Probe ~88% and the cost framing.** The framing is honest and the "not surprising in the stop-gradient arm" concession is right. One addition would sharpen it: the λ=1 arm *does* deliver label information to W₁ (CE gradient present, just outweighed 20–50×) and still probes at 88.4 — essentially identical to stop-gradient's 88.35. That's a stronger statement than the section draws out: even with label gradient flowing, dominance determines the representation, not just the conditioning. That's your graded claim operating on *features*, not only on spread, and it deserves its own sentence.

5. **Future Strengthening appears twice** — here and in Setup, with overlapping content. Consolidate into one place (my earlier suggestion stands: a limitations/future-work discussion, not inside setup or results). And within it, the same triage as before: the denser λ curve and Adam-stop-gradient arm are pre-submission items — the text itself calls the λ-ratio curve "the central figure for the graded claim," which is an odd thing to say about a figure that doesn't exist. Either make it or demote the phrasing.

"mostly along for the ride" — fine, earns its keep. The verdict column's bare checkmarks could be "supported" for register. Otherwise the section's discipline (diagnostic vs. matched measurement, bound-forfeiture vs. largeness) is the best in the paper.
