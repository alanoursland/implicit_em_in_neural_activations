This results section is well-constructed — the arms are read as answers to questions rather than rows in a table, and the variance-only pathology is the most convincing finding so far because it's a *prediction-shaped* result (partial repair worse than none is exactly what the mixture analogy implies, and it's not the result generic-regularization skeptics would expect). Notes:

**The argument**

1. **The Var+Decorr-only control is doing its job, but the interpretation leans on small deltas.** The claim "not the same mechanism" rests on entropy 2.65 vs 2.46 and min-var 3.55 vs 7.84. The min-var gap looks real (it's ~2σ+ by the reported spreads). The entropy gap of 0.19 with seed SDs of 0.07 and 0.04 is also plausibly real, but here's the interpretive exposure: *entropy is only meaningful in the NLS-comparable sense if r = softmax(−d) is a calibrated responsibility in both arms* — and in the Var+Decorr-only arm there's no NLS, so computing softmax(−d) over raw ReLU outputs is measuring sharpness of a quantity the model was never trained to calibrate. You're comparing a trained responsibility against an untrained pseudo-responsibility. That's defensible as a descriptive statistic, but the sentence "do not create the same calibrated distance-to-responsibility interface" is currently supported by construction (of course they don't — NLS *is* the calibration) rather than by the numbers. I'd restructure: the architectural point (no NLS → no calibrated interface) needs no evidence; the empirical point is the min-var difference and, if you have it, a downstream difference — does the mechanism distinction *matter*? A linear probe on d for these two arms would answer that in one row. If probe accuracy is identical, the honest reading is "same practical effect, different formal structure," which is fine but should be said plainly.

2. **The two-regimes accuracy story is honest but has an untested implication.** You attribute the large-K accuracy dip to fixed λ being miscalibrated per width. That's a claim with a cheap test: rerun K=64 with a smaller λ and show accuracy recovers while dead units stay at zero. Without it, a reviewer can offer the rival reading — volume control's structural health is intrinsically accuracy-costly at scale — and the text can't rule it out. One run (or even a 3-point λ mini-sweep at K=64) converts a plausible excuse into a demonstrated mechanism. If that run exists in your logs, cite it; if not, this is the cheapest high-value addition in the section. Also, "larger models can use correlated extra units as boundary refiners" is a nice mechanistic story presented as fact — either point to evidence (e.g., correlated-unit clusters aligning with confusable digit pairs) or mark it as interpretation.

3. **Missing uncertainty in Table 2.** Min Var and Accuracy in the capacity table have no ± while Dead and Redundancy do. With 5 seeds, accuracy differences of 0.1–0.3% need SDs to be interpretable — especially since the two-regimes claim depends on the sign of small accuracy deltas at K=49/64. Add the SDs; if 96.87 vs 97.04 overlaps at 1σ, "slightly hurts" should soften to "is statistically indistinguishable or slightly worse."

4. **Dead-unit counting under NLS+Var arms.** Zero dead units across all VC arms and widths is a strong invariant — strong enough that a reviewer will ask whether the variance *penalty* mechanically guarantees it (a component can't have near-zero variance if the loss directly punishes near-zero variance; the metric and the training objective share a definition). It's still a real result — the point is that the repair works — but one sentence acknowledging the metric-objective coupling, plus one metric *not* directly optimized (e.g., responsibility mass share of the weakest component, or probe-relevant unit ablation) would immunize it. The redundancy result doesn't have this problem in the decorr arms... except it does, symmetrically: redundancy is the decorr training target. The cleanest untrained metric you already have is responsibility entropy; consider foregrounding it, or report the cross-metrics (does Var-only reduce *redundancy*? Table 1 says no — 187 — which is actually your best evidence that these metrics aren't trivially co-optimized; make that point explicitly).

5. **Small consistency issues between tables.** K=25 baseline redundancy is 20.5±2.8 in Table 1 but 19.0±1.3 in Table 2; accuracy 96.09 vs 96.07; dead 1.40±0.49 vs 1.4±0.5. These are presumably 10-seed vs 5-seed runs of the same config — say so in the Table 2 caption, or a reviewer will flag the discrepancy as sloppiness rather than seed count.

6. **"This is the cleanest replication of the decoder-free encoder ablation inside a supervised network"** — good sentence, and replication across settings is worth more emphasis than it gets; consider one clause stating what the single-layer numbers were (or a pointer), so "replication" is checkable.
