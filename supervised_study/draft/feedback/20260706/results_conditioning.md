The section's logic is sound — observational boundary here, causal test deferred, and the final subsection saying exactly that is the right epistemics. The main issues are in what the table quietly reveals and what comparison is missing.

**The argument**

1. **The learning-rate sensitivity shown is one-sided, and it's the less interesting side.** Look at the SGD column: accuracy *increases monotonically* through 10⁻¹, and the best SGD setting is the largest learning rate tested. Every failure in the sweep is a too-slow failure (10⁻⁴ hasn't converged by epoch 50), not an instability failure. But the theory's forfeited guarantee is a *smoothness* story — it predicts the loss of a safe upper stability range, i.e., blowups at large η, and the sweep never finds the blowup. So the claimed "learning-rate axis is active again" is currently supported by convergence-speed sensitivity, which even a perfectly conditioned problem exhibits when η is small. Two fixes, either sufficient: (a) extend the SGD sweep upward until it destabilizes, so the sensitivity has both edges; (b) make the comparison explicit against the single-layer model's numbers — the prior claim was that convergence time was roughly *fixed* across a wide η range, so the diagnostic here should be "time to X% accuracy vs η," not final accuracy. Right now the section asserts the signature disappeared without showing the signature's original shape next to the new one. A small table or figure with single-layer vs composed spread (same metric, same η grid) would carry the whole section.

2. **Missing control: the plain baseline's optimizer sweep.** The claim is "the composed model behaves like an ordinary network." That needs the ordinary network in the table — baseline (no NLS, no VC) under the same sweep. Almost certainly it looks the same, which is exactly the point: the composed EM-site model is indistinguishable from a vanilla classifier in conditioning. Without that row, a skeptic can wonder whether the auxiliary terms *caused* the sensitivity rather than failing to prevent it.

3. **The variance explosion is a finding wearing a footnote's clothing.** Min var 30628, reg loss −265.9: the auxiliary objective is unbounded below, and Adam at high η happily rides that direction. I'd flag two things. First, the likely mechanism is sitting in your architecture: LayerNorm after W₂ makes downstream CE invariant to the scale of the intermediate representation, so variance can grow without bound at zero accuracy cost — LN creates the flat direction the variance term escapes along. If that's right (check whether removing LN or adding a variance *ceiling* stops the runaway), it's worth two sentences, because it answers "why doesn't the task loss push back?" mechanistically instead of leaving "orthogonal degrees of freedom" as an assertion. Second — and this connects to the volume-control-curvature IOU from the theory section — an objective that is unbounded below with a runaway direction is relevant to the *single-layer* conditioning story too: the clean claim "the smoothness constant is fixed before training" is about L_LSE, while the trained objective contains terms that can be driven to −∞. The single-layer model evidently didn't run away (why? bounded by what — the LSE term's opposition, finite training, implicit η limits?). One paragraph somewhere reconciling this would close the last theory-experiment gap I've been tracking. Low-confidence guess at the resolution: in the single-layer model there's no LN and no head, so variance growth *does* change L_LSE and gets opposed; the runaway direction is created by composition, which would actually be a nice bonus result — composition doesn't just forfeit conditioning, it manufactures a degenerate direction. Worth checking whether your logs support that reading.

4. **Table caption gaps.** "Reg." needs a definition and an explanation of negative values (readers will assume a loss is nonnegative and suspect a typo at −265.9). Min Var and Redundancy lack ±. And state that CE/Reg are train-set values (I assume) while accuracy is test.

5. **"Adam at 10⁻³ is therefore the sensible operating point"** — reasonable, but note it's being selected partly *because* it avoids a pathology of your own auxiliary objective. That's fine; just say it that way ("we operate below the runaway regime") rather than as pure hyperparameter housekeeping, since Results III's headline ratios are measured at this point.
