Related, smaller: the a.e. ReLU check draws random θ and finite-differences with eps = 1e−4; if a preactivation lands within ~eps of a kink, the numerical Hessian will see the kink and can spuriously violate the bound. Your 1e−3 tolerance is probably absorbing this, but it'd be cleaner to reject draws with min|z| < 10·eps.

**3. prop1.py's "up to sign" min() papers over a determinate sign.** `err = min(|H_kron − Hn|, |−H_kron − Hn|)` — but the sign isn't ambiguous: Lemma 1 says the Hessian is rrᵀ − diag(r) = −C(r), so ∇²_θL = −C(r) ⊗ x̃x̃ᵀ exactly. The min() means a sign error in the paper's stated Hessian would still PASS. Fix the sign in H_kron and drop the min. This matters a little more than it looks, because the lemma's sign convention (Hessian vs. negative-Hessian-as-covariance) is one of the easier things to garble in the LaTeX.
